# The Tiny Cat Who Rode an Axolotl

Mimsy was the smallest cat in the whole house, small enough to nap in a teacup and small enough to be stepped over by the dog.

She was tired of being stepped over.

One evening, she crept to the edge of the big pond in the garden, where a pale pink axolotl named Olo drifted beneath the lily pads, smiling his permanent smile.

"Excuse me," said Mimsy. "I need to get to the other side, and I don't like water."

"Then you've come to the wrong pond," said Olo, gills fluttering. He paused, and his smile grew kinder. "But I'd be glad to help. Climb on, and I promise I won't let you fall."

"I've come to the right axolotl," said Mimsy, and hopped onto his back.

Olo considered this. Nobody had ever ridden him before. It felt oddly nice, like wearing a very small, very warm hat.

"Hold on," he said gently, "I'll keep it nice and steady."

Mimsy's paws stayed dry, because Olo swam just under the surface of the shallows, tilting carefully around every ripple so she never wobbled. He checked over his shoulder often. "Still comfortable?" he asked. "Perfectly," said Mimsy, and she clung to him like a sailor on a pink and slippery ship. They sailed past a grumpy frog, who forgot to croak. They sailed under a dragonfly, who swore it hadn't seen anything. Mimsy lifted her chin and felt, for the first time, enormous.

At the far bank, a single firefly sat on a reed. Mimsy had wanted to see it up close for weeks.

"Worth it?" asked Olo.

"Worth it," whispered Mimsy. "You are the best, kindest, most dependable axolotl in the whole world."

"I'm just glad you asked," said Olo, and he meant it.

They stayed until the stars came out. Then the tiny cat climbed back aboard, and the axolotl carried her home across the dark water, the two of them leaving a quiet silver ripple behind.

In the morning the dog stepped over Mimsy again. She just yawned. She knew something the dog didn't: she had the finest steed in the garden, and a true friend besides.

*The End*
